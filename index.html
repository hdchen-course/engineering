📖 Preface

This handbook collects the material you need to walk into a senior-level technical loop with confidence. It spans the two pillars every FAANG-style interview tests — coding under time pressure and designing systems that scale — plus the day-to-day craft that separates strong engineers from great ones.

Who it's for: software engineers preparing for coding tests and system-design rounds at large technology companies, and anyone who wants a structured, concise refresher on the fundamentals. Each chapter is written to stand alone, so you can read cover-to-cover or jump straight to a gap.

🧭 How to use this book
  • Diagnose first. Skim the Table of Contents and mark the topics you can't explain out loud. Those are your starting points.
  • Follow a track. Pick the Learning Track that matches your interview (coding, system design, or craft) and read its parts in order.
  • Practice actively. The coding chapters pair with the ramp-up plans in Part III — read a pattern, then drill it against a timed problem set.
  • Rehearse aloud. For design and craft chapters, close the page and re-derive the key trade-offs from memory. If you can teach it, you know it.

Learning Tracks

Three curated reading paths. Follow the parts in the order shown.

Coding-Test Track

Parts I–III, X · for algorithm & data-structure rounds
  1. Part I — Build the toolkit: complexity, patterns, recursion, and interview math (Ch. 1–5).
  2. Part II — Work the data structures & algorithms in order, arrays through advanced topics (Ch. 1–11).
  3. Part III — Lock it in with a timed ramp-up plan: 1, 2, or 4 weeks (Ch. 1–3).
  4. Part X — Drill curated Google-style problems with worked, execution-tested solutions and interviewer probes (Ch. 1–12).

System-Design Track

Parts IV–VII, XI–XV · for architecture & design rounds
  1. Part IV — Fundamentals & the interview playbook, plus observability, operational excellence, worked designs, and the Google papers (Ch. 1–7).
  2. Part V — Data infrastructure: how to choose a datastore and the internals of six of them (Ch. 1–7).
  3. Part VI — AI/ML systems: RAG, real-time assist, GPU clouds, TPU supercomputers, and ML cluster SWE (Ch. 1–11). Chapters 2–8 form a focused contact-center/agent-assist track.
  4. Part VII — Security systems design, from authorization and transport security to memory safety, device provisioning, and secure OTA (Ch. 1–10).
  5. Part XI — Practice bank: classic system-design problems worked end-to-end — rate limiter, feed, notifications, payments, code judge, URL shortener, unique ID, video platform (Ch. 1–8).
  6. Part XII — Real-world engineering case studies drawn from public engineering blogs and postmortems (Ch. 1–4).
  7. Part XIII — Systems, networking & performance deep-dives: Kubernetes, TCP/IP, low-latency C++, WiFi, and cloud & data-center networking (Ch. 1–5).
  8. Part XIV — Applied & emerging system design: device lab, agent workflows, mobile telemetry (Ch. 1–3).
  9. Part XV — Server systems software & firmware: the boot chain & root of trust, the Linux kernel/hardware boundary, low-level observability, and node-level orchestration & fleet bring-up (Ch. 1–4).

Craft Track

Parts VIII–IX · for the whole-engineer signal
  1. Part VIII — Sharpen the craft: code review, language refreshers (C, C++, Go, Rust, Kotlin, Objective-C, Swift, Java, Python, Shell), and working effectively with AI-assisted tooling (Ch. 1–12).
  2. Part IX — Mobile engineering: Android, iOS, and mobile system design for platform-specific rounds (Ch. 1–3).
  3. Read alongside either track — these skills surface in behavioral, code-review, and hands-on rounds.
